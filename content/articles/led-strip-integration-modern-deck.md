## Quick Takeaways

- Outdoor LED strips need **IP65 or higher** — indoor strips will fail
- **COB (Chip-on-Board)** produces seamless light with no visible dots
- **220V strips** need an electrician but run up to 50 meters without voltage drop
- **12V/24V strips** are DIY-friendly with a plug-in transformer
- **Solar strips** need no wiring but depend on direct sunlight
- **Never install strips on stair treads** — they will be crushed. Use step lights instead.

## The Three Questions You Must Answer First

Before buying any LED strip, answer these three questions. Most mistakes happen when people skip this step.

**1. Do you have power nearby?**

| Your situation | Go to this product |
|----------------|---------------------|
| No power source nearby (remote garden, shed, rental property) | Solar SMD strip |
| Yes, and hiring an electrician (new build, major renovation) | 220V COB strip |
| Yes, and have an outdoor outlet (DIY) | 12V/24V DC COB strip |

**2. Will the strip be visible or hidden?**
- Visible (under rail, stair riser, pergola beam) → **COB only** (no dots)
- Hidden (inside channel, under bench, behind fascia) → SMD is acceptable

**3. Will the strip get wet?**
- Rain only → IP65
- Direct hose spray or splashing → IP67
- Pond or water feature → IP68 (not covered in this guide)

## Where to Install LED Strips (And Where to Avoid)

LED strips excel in specific locations. Use them here:

| Location | Why It Works |
|----------|--------------|
| Under cap rails | Lights the deck surface from above — invisible from seating view |
| Stair risers (vertical face) | Creates floating tread effect — safe and dramatic |
| Under bench seats | Grazes the deck surface below |
| Inside aluminum channels | Protects strip and diffuses light. Aluminum channels also improve heat dissipation and protect the strip from accidental impact, extending its lifespan. |
| Along pergola beams | Defines overhead structure |
| Behind outdoor kitchen fascia | Indirect accent lighting |

## Three Places You Should NOT Use LED Strips

Knowing when to say no to a strip is more important than knowing where to put one.

**1. On stair treads (the horizontal surface you step on)**

Strips will be crushed, kicked, and destroyed within weeks. The adhesive fails, the silicone tears, and water gets in. Instead, use solar step lights mounted on the vertical riser or side of the tread.

## COB vs SMD: The Dot Problem Explained

This is the single biggest difference nobody explains.

| | COB (Chip-on-Board) | SMD (Surface-Mount Device) |
|---|---|---|
| **Visible dots** | None — continuous light line | Yes — individual LEDs visible |
| **Best for** | Exposed installations (under rails, stair risers) | Hidden installations (inside channels, behind facias) |
| **LED density** | 240-480 LEDs/m | 60-120 LEDs/m |
| **Thickness** | Thin (9.5×5mm silicone tube) | Thin to medium |
| **Available in solar?** | No | Yes |

**Bottom line:** If the strip is visible, buy COB. If it's hidden, SMD is fine and often cheaper.

## IP65 vs IP67: What Waterproofing Actually Means

| Rating | What it survives | Where to use |
|--------|------------------|--------------|
| IP65 | Low-pressure water jets, rain, hose spray | Covered decks, under eaves, most outdoor applications |
| IP67 | Temporary immersion up to 1 meter | Steps, ground-level, areas with pooling water |
| IP68 | Continuous immersion | Ponds, fountains (not covered here) |

While outdoor LED strips should have a minimum IP65 rating, all three strips featured in this guide are IP67 for additional protection. The step lights, ground lights, and wall lights are IP65 — fine for rain but don't submerge them.

## Installation Quick Start

**For 12V/24V strips:**
1. Clean surface with isopropyl alcohol (adhesive won't stick to dust)
2. Plan your transformer location (must be within 1.5m of outlet)
3. Test strip before peeling adhesive
4. Peel and press firmly along entire length
5. Add mounting clips every 30cm for permanent security

**For solar strips:**
1. Position solar panel for maximum direct sunlight:
   - **Northern Hemisphere** → south-facing
   - **Southern Hemisphere** → north-facing
2. Keep panel wire runs under 5 meters
3. Fully charge before first use (24-36 hours in sun)

**Tools needed:**
- Isopropyl alcohol and clean rag
- Scissors (for cutting strips at marked lines)
- Soldering kit or quick-connect clips (for corners)
- Waterproof junction box (for connections)
- Mounting clips (adhesive alone will fail eventually)

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Can I use any LED strip outdoors?**  
No. Outdoor strips need a minimum IP65 waterproof rating. Standard indoor strips will fail in rain, humidity, or even morning dew.

**What's the difference between COB and SMD LED strips?**  
COB produces a continuous line of light with no visible dots between LEDs. SMD strips have visible gaps. For exposed installations like under railings, always choose COB.

**Do I need an electrician for LED strip installation?**  
For 220V strips — yes. For 12V/24V DC strips and solar strips — no, they are DIY-friendly with plug-in transformers or solar panels.

**How long do outdoor LED strips last?**  
Quality strips last 25,000-50,000 hours. That's 10-20 years of evening use. Solar strips depend on battery health, typically 2-3 years before replacement.

**Can I cut LED strips to length?**  
Yes — all three strips in this guide are cuttable. 220V and 12V/24V COB strips cut every 50cm or 25cm. Solar SMD strips cut every 5cm.

**What color temperature should I choose for outdoor lighting?**  
Warm white (2700K-3000K) creates an inviting, residential feel. Natural white (4000-4500K) works for task lighting. Avoid cool white (6000K+) for social spaces.

**Can LED strips be installed on stairs?**  
Not on the tread surface — they will be crushed. Install them on stair risers (vertical face) or use dedicated step lights instead.

**Do solar LED strips work in winter?**  
Yes, but with shorter runtime. Shorter daylight hours and lower sun angles reduce charging. Position the solar panel for maximum direct sunlight.

**Can I connect multiple LED strips together?**  
For 12V/24V strips, yes — but each connection adds resistance. For runs over 10m, use a power injector or feed from both ends. For 220V strips, an electrician must handle all connections. Solar strips cannot be daisy-chained.
